# Thumbnail & Title

Thumbnail + title are one unit: together they make one promise, each carrying half of it. Design the thumbnail *before* rendering the video — if the promise changes, the hook changes.

## Thumbnail Design Rules

- **Contrast first:** subject must separate from background at a glance — bright subject/dark bg or reverse, plus a stroke/outline on the subject. Test in greyscale: if it merges, fix it.
- **3-word rule:** max 3 words on the thumbnail (often 0–2). If the title says it, the thumbnail doesn't repeat it.
- **One subject, big:** main element fills 60–80% of frame. Faces: close-cropped, eyes visible, exaggerated-but-honest expression (surprise/curiosity outperform neutral or fake shock).
- **Colour clash:** use a complementary clash against the platform's white/dark UI and against competing thumbnails — e.g. yellow/cyan on dark, red accent on teal. Brand colour is secondary to clash.
- **Mobile legibility test:** shrink to 5% size / phone width (~360 px). Text and subject must still read. If not: fewer words, bigger subject, more contrast.
- **Safe placement:** keep key elements out of the bottom-right (duration badge) and don't rely on edges (rounded-corner crops).
- **Honesty:** the thumbnail must depict something the video actually delivers. Mismatched thumbnails tank retention and trust.
- Faces looking at camera (or at the object) outperform faces looking away. Add an arrow/circle only if it points at a real payoff — decoration arrows are noise.
- Bengali text on thumbnails: bold Hind Siliguri / Noto Sans Bengali, test conjunct rendering at small size; prefer short words, avoid long conjunct clusters at <48 px.

## Title Formulas (12)

1. `How to [outcome] (without [pain])` — "How to Edit Videos (Without a PC)"
2. `[Number] [Things] That [benefit/mistake]` — "7 Caption Mistakes Killing Your Views"
3. `I Tried [X] for [time] — Here's What Happened`
4. `[X] vs [Y]: Which Is Actually Better?`
5. `Stop Doing [X]. Do This Instead.`
6. `The Truth About [X] Nobody Tells You`
7. `[Outcome] in [timeframe] — Full Process`
8. `Why [surprising claim] (And How to Fix It)`
9. `[Beginner/Pro] Guide to [topic] — [year]` (year only if genuinely updated)
10. `আপনার [X] কেন [problem]? এই ভুলটা থামান` — BN curiosity + fix promise
11. `I Built [thing] With [constraint]` — "I Built a House Estimate in 10 Minutes"
12. `[Big number/stat] — [short explanation tease]`

Title rules: ≤60 characters visible where possible; keyword/topic in the first half; no ALL CAPS full titles; no clickbait the video doesn't pay off; brackets/parentheses for one qualifier max `[FULL GUIDE]`.

## CTR Pitfalls

- Thumbnail repeats the title word-for-word (wasted space, no added curiosity).
- Busy collage of 5+ elements; viewer can't parse it in 1 second.
- Tiny text designed at full-screen monitor size, never checked on mobile.
- Same template/face/colours on every upload — regulars go blind to it. Refresh layout every 5–10 videos.
- Promising a number/result the video buries at the end or never shows.
- Dark, low-contrast thumbnails that vanish in dark-mode UI.

## A/B Testing Workflow

1. Make 2–3 variants differing in **one** variable only (face vs no face, or word choice, or colour clash).
2. YouTube: use "Test & compare" (Studio) when available — it needs up to 2 weeks / enough impressions; judge on watch-time share, not CTR alone.
3. Without native testing: change nothing mid-flight on a performing video; test on the *next* similar upload and log results.
4. Keep a simple log: date, video, variant, CTR %, avg view duration. Decide on ≥1,000 impressions where possible.
5. Promote the winning *pattern* (not the exact image) into the channel's style notes / learned style skill.
